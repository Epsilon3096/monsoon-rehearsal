## Fifteen-minute review

1. Before showing results, ask: “When a storm may arrive before all work is finished, how do you choose jobs today? What would you do with one crew and a short work window?” Record the answer before explaining our policy.
2. Show the guided demonstration: outlet restoration, bounded work window, then the original B2 adverse case. Explain that both networks are synthetic and neither is a validated site model.
3. Ask whether the assumed work durations, access, travel, stop conditions and return margin are plausible. Can an interrupted job leave a more dangerous condition than before? Which work must be indivisible or have an explicit safe-stop state?
4. Ask what observations are required to confirm blockage, downstream water level and post-work condition. Who can authorise and stop work?
5. Ask which useful decisions are missing and whether a read-only shadow trial would be worthwhile. “No” and corrections are useful outcomes.

## Minimum next evidence

A settled data contract needs topology, levels and geometry, observed restrictions, receiving-water boundaries, maintenance durations and safe-stop states. A prospective shadow trial must record the operator's decision and model recommendation before outcomes are known. Preserve disagreements and failures. A supervised pilot requires appropriate site authority and professional review; an interview alone does not provide either.
