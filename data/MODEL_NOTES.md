# Synthetic hydraulic reference specification

The geometry and nine scenario definitions in `reference_case.json` were fixed before any optimiser or baseline performance comparison. The first executed case was P1 with no completed jobs, solely to establish successful engine execution and unit/continuity checks. Any later geometry change must create a new model version and be explained; scenarios must not be tuned to manufacture an optimiser win.

The reference represents two prescribed inflow hydrographs in m3/s, seven conduits and eight nodes. Branch A drains directly to confluence J. Branch B passes through a designed 300 m2 constant-area storage S with a fixed 0.5 m circular outlet to J. The shared rectangular trunk discharges to an ungated receiving-water boundary; reverse flow is permitted. The artificial coordinate datum and schematic coordinates have no surveyed geographic meaning.

Jobs J1–J4 independently restore rectangular clear width only. These are explicit synthetic lateral encroachments, not sediment-depth estimates, capacity percentages derived from photographs, or inlet-capture restoration. Bottom levels, conduit lengths, roughness and full depths remain fixed. The designed storage outlet never changes. J3 acts upstream of B2; J4 acts between B2 and S. They cannot duplicate the same physical task.

All node flooding crosses an absorbing collector boundary once. `ALLOW_PONDING NO` prevents re-entry; the objective is represented drainage spill, not a prediction of road depth, houses protected, inundation extent or economic damage. Every non-outfall node is protected against spill worsening. Threshold-duration is a supplementary diagnostic at 80% of node maximum depth, with a piecewise-linear crossing calculation at each routing step.

All states represent completed, verified maintenance before a prescribed 180-minute hydraulic experiment. The scheduler's work deadline is separate from that simulation horizon. Maintenance history during wet conditions is unsupported. SWMM initial numerical link wetting and initial storage are included explicitly in mass balance. A high fixed tailwater can introduce water from the receiving boundary; it is not counted as prescribed runoff.

P4 and D1 deliberately begin without an antecedent warm-up: junction initial depths and conduit initial flows are zero, the fixed receiving stage is imposed at simulation start, and SWMM initialises link water consistently with its start-up rules. These are non-equilibrated cold-start experiments. Their early backflow and peak levels include the resulting filling transient; they do not represent a network already equilibrated to an observed receiving-water level. Restored geometry can change initial link volume, and each run records that volume rather than assuming equal initial volume for all states.

H2's 1.25 m initial storage depth is a synthetic prescribed scenario condition. No initial level in this reference was observed in the field. The frozen case's early assumption label mentioning an "observed hydraulic state" is an architectural description, not a measurement claim; the implemented case uses prescribed initial conditions. A site application would require measured or justified antecedent states and a reviewed initialisation procedure.

The default engine uses SWMM 5.2.4, toolkit 0.17.0, dynamic-wave routing, a fixed 0.5-second routing step, 12 trials and 0.0001 m head tolerance. The test suite halves the routing step to 0.25 seconds. Initial verification at 1 second passed continuity but failed the declared 2% spill convergence target in eight D1 reverse-tailwater states. For D1 without work, spill was 709.61 m3 at 1 second, 689.59 at 0.5 seconds, 688.46 at 0.25 seconds and 688.19 at 0.125 seconds. The default step was therefore reduced globally before acceptance; no geometry or scenario was changed. Continuity must remain below 1%, independent balance must meet 1% (0.1 m3 absolute near zero), node spill totals must reconcile to routing totals, and every node must converge. Failed/invalid runs have no usable score. A custom routing step outside the exhaustively verified default pair is an experiment, not a certified numerical setting.

Each run saves its exact `.inp`, native `.rpt` and `.out`, result JSON, worker log and hashed manifest. Physical input, the complete case snapshot (including metric definitions), and engine/interface/adapter fingerprints determine the result-cache ID. The engine fingerprint includes the installed native binaries and relevant Python interface files, not only version labels. Changing a call's scenario label or crew durations does not influence the hydraulics cache. Runs use separate subprocesses, and repeated attempts never overwrite raw evidence. Missing, structurally corrupt or inconsistent cache evidence causes a fresh attempt. Storage and outfall geometry are read from the frozen case fields rather than duplicated constants in the adapter.

The horizon review extends each frozen state/scenario pair from 180 to 360 minutes. Event-spill adequacy is a gate for planning and holdout cases; diagnostic boundary controls are reported separately. D1 remains horizon-sensitive: with no work its spill is 689.59 m3 by 180 minutes and 822.75 m3 by 360 minutes. That is a timed boundary-startup result, not a complete-event spill total. An independent D1 variant with the same geometry and 100.8 m stage but hydrostatic initial node levels has zero inflow, outflow and spill over both horizons. The original diagnostic case remains frozen; the variant explains the importance of initial conditions rather than replacing an unfavourable result. See `evidence/horizon.json`, `evidence/initial-state-review.json` and `evidence/hydraulics-review.md`.

The toolkit source converts accumulated flow and node statistics to selected user units. Its routing-total initial/final storage fields retain internal ft3 in the installed version; the adapter instead sums node and link VOLUME APIs in m3 and verifies that balance against engine continuity. Routing-step time is returned in days by the engine despite a legacy toolkit docstring saying milliseconds. Both choices have direct verification tests.

P1–P4 are planning cases. H1 (double pulse) and H2 (plateau plus initially filled storage) are mechanism-family holdouts that are never inputs to the planning UI. D0–D2 isolate dry, reverse-tailwater and outlet-dominated conditions. They are deterministic synthetic scenarios, not occurrence probabilities. They provide engineering software verification, not real-world calibration or validation.
